Your session's model handles the conversation, the decisions, and every step that needs you. When a GAIA workflow hands a step to a [sub-agent](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents) (a separate Claude instance with its own fresh context), GAIA picks the sub-agent's model for that kind of work. This page lists every one of those choices.

## How GAIA picks a model

GAIA ranks models by how well they fit the work: how much reasoning it needs, and how closely the model has to stick to its instructions. Price decides only between two models that fit equally well.

| Kind of work | Model | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Pulling out, sorting, or listing items by a written rule | Haiku | A rule decides each item, and the result can be counted or checked. |
| Following a template, a rule id, or a written convention: scaffolding, or a fix for a specific lint or accessibility rule | Haiku | The template already holds the answer. More reasoning adds nothing. |
| Reading widely and making a call on each item: research, checking a finding, verifying | Sonnet | The instructions carry the judgment, and Sonnet applies it one item at a time without straying from them. |
| A bounded change with a written plan and a check behind it | Sonnet | The plan carries the judgment, and Sonnet sticks to the plan. |
| Review: finding what a change got wrong | Opus | It needs sustained judgment and a wide view. A missed defect costs more than the price difference. |
| Specs, plans, and design or architecture decisions | Opus (or Fable) | One weak choice here carries into everything built on it. |

<Aside type="note" title="GAIA never chooses Fable on its own">
A step runs on Fable only when your session is on Fable and the step uses your model, or when you pick Fable at `/gaia-plan`'s planner question.
</Aside>

Some steps never leave your session: a step that asks you something (a sub-agent cannot), small work, and quick edit-run-fix loops, where handing off costs more than it saves. For the roles sub-agents take and why only your session commits, see [Who does what](/workflow/work-lifecycle/#who-does-what).

### Effort

GAIA sets no effort, with one exception. Every sub-agent runs at your session's `/effort` level, the same as work your session does itself, so a low-effort session reviews at low and a high-effort one at high. The exception is the [Code Audit Team](/workflow/code-audit-team/)'s [code review](/workflow/code-audit-team/#the-code-review), which always runs `/code-review` at its `high` level. That level is `/code-review`'s own depth setting, not `/effort`. The `CLAUDE_CODE_EFFORT_LEVEL` environment variable overrides the level on a machine. Set it in your shell profile, then restart Claude Code. GAIA chooses the model. You choose the effort, except for the code review.

## Skills that set a model

Six skills set Haiku in their frontmatter: the four scaffolders (`new-component`, `new-route`, `new-hook`, `new-service`) and the two fix skills (`eslint-fixes`, `a11y-fixes`). When one loads, Claude Code runs the rest of that turn on Haiku, which suits a turn that only follows a template or fixes one rule.

Every other skill runs on your model. Some, such as `react-code` and `tailwind`, load in the middle of other work, so a Haiku setting would move the rest of your implementation turn to Haiku.

Set a model on a skill you write only when every use of it is template or rule-id work. A pinned skill runs the rest of the turn on its model, even when you picked a stronger one.

## What you control

- **Your session's model**, with `/model`. It runs every step that is not listed in the table, and everything that needs you.
- **The planner's model**, when `/gaia-plan` asks.
- **Effort**, with `/effort`, for your session and every sub-agent.

What each step costs shows up in the [cost and usage ledger](/models-and-context/cost/).
